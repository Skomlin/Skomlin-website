Jean-Michel Olivier's L'Enfant secret won the Swiss Prix Dentan. In her introduction to this edition, Clorinda Donato calls it “a profoundly personal exploration of those secrets we all inherit almost as part of our DNA”: the hidden deeds of ancestors we glimpse only through “the inadvertent confession of a distant cousin or a box of photos suddenly unearthed.”

The narrator traces two sets of grandparents: Julien and Emilie in the villages of Vaud, and Antonio and Nora in Trieste as the city passed from Austrian to Italian rule. James and Nora Joyce, Ezra Pound and Vladimir Nabokov make brief appearances. At the centre is his maternal grandfather, born Anton Buchacher, who remade himself as the photographer Antonio Campofaggi and whose images helped turn Benito Mussolini into Il Duce.

Laurence Moscato's translation keeps the distance between Swiss French and the French of France, the distance that, as Donato writes, “makes the text Swiss.”

Contributors

Jean-Michel OlivierAuthor
Jean-Michel Olivier (born 1952 in Nyon, Vaud) is a Swiss novelist, journalist and teacher who studied literature at the University of Geneva under Jean Starobinski and Michel Butor. The author of some twenty novels alongside essays and poetry, he won the Prix Interallie in 2010 for L'Amour negre - a rare Swiss recipient of a major French literary prize. Since 2006 he has directed the Poche Suisse collection at Editions L'Age d'Homme.
Laurence MoscatoTranslator
Laurence Moscato is a professor of translation studies in California and a literary translator working from French into English. Her English translation of Jean-Michel Olivier's Prix Dentan-winning L'Enfant secret, published as The Secret Child (Skomlin, 2018) with a preface by the scholar Clorinda Donato, was praised for its deft rendering of the author's lyrical prose.
