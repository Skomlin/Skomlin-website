The Seven Who Were Hanged

Andreyev depicts the fates of five failed leftist revolutionaries and two common peasants who have received death sentences. The condemned await execution by hanging. In prison each of the prisoners deals with his fate in his or her own way.

This is the book that inspired a group of Bosnian revolutionaries to assassinate Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggering the crisis that led to World War I.

Contributors

Leonid AndreyevAuthor
Leonid Andreyev (1871-1919) was a Russian writer and playwright, a leading figure of the Silver Age whose intense, often bleak work bridged realism and expressionism. Born in Oryol, he won early fame with stories confronting death, fear and social violence; The Seven Who Were Hanged (1908) is his best-known tale.
